What makes a good flipbook clip

A flipbook is tiny, held in a hand, and seen for a second or two. AI video tools love sweeping cameras and busy detail — both fight you here. Ask for:

  • A locked camera. No pans, zooms, or cuts. If the camera moves, the whole picture shifts on every page and the motion turns to mush. Video models tend to add motion to everything unless told otherwise, so say it outright.
  • One subject, one action. A short clip has room for one beat. Two or three things happening at once read as noise on a 2-inch slip.
  • Think in three beats: starting pose → the change → the ending pose. It keeps the model from scattering the motion.
  • Plain, still background and strong contrast. Bold shapes and thick lines survive printing and small sizes; fine texture, small text, and flickering light don't.
  • Side view, centered, fully in frame. Sideways motion (running, jumping, bouncing) reads best. Keep the subject from leaving the edge.
  • 2–4 seconds. At 12 frames a second that's 24–48 slips — a book that's satisfying and still easy to bind.
  • Match your slip shape. Landscape (3:2 or 16:9) suits the slip layouts here.
  • Loops: asking for “end matches start” helps, but it isn't a promise — check the first and last frames and trim if they jump.
  • Iterate one change at a time. Look at the first result, find the worst problem, fix only that instruction.
Easiest path for crisp results: ask an AI to draw the animation as code (the “Draw it with Claude” box). You get clean lines, an exact loop, and perfect frame control — no video tool needed.

Before you print

  • Paper: thin copy paper bends and sticks together. Use heavy paper or light cardstock — stiff enough to spring back, not so stiff it won't bend under your thumb. Print one test sheet first.
  • Scale: print at 100% (“Actual size”).
  • How many slips: about 12 a second of animation. More slips with smaller changes look smoother.
  • Pages fill evenly when your slip count divides by the slips-per-page number shown on the Make tab.

Cut, stack, bind

  1. Cut along the gray lines. Slips sit edge to edge, so one cut separates two slips. A paper trimmer or craft knife with a ruler keeps edges true — straight edges flip far better.
  2. Stack in number order with the cover on top, slip 1 under it, and the highest number at the bottom, back card last. All pictures face up.
  3. Square the stack. Tap the binding edge and the flipping edge on the table until every slip lines up. Misaligned slips make the picture jitter.
  4. Bind the strip side (the shaded edge with the numbers): a strong binder clip is easiest; or glue each slip to the one below along the strip, or wrap the strip tightly in tape. For a sturdier book, glue the back card onto a piece of thick card.
  5. Flip: hold the binding in one hand, bend the free edge with your thumb, and let the slips slide off one at a time from the front. With the picture on the right, hold in your left hand and flip with your right thumb (and the reverse for picture left).

Two-sided books

  1. Print double-sided. The PDF alternates front and back pages. Choose double-sided in the print dialog and flip on the long edge for portrait sheets or the short edge for landscape (each sheet’s footer tells you). Print one test sheet and hold it to the light: every back picture should sit behind its front slip.
  2. Cut and stack as above. Thin paper shows the other side through it, so use heavy paper or light cardstock for double-sided books.
  3. Flip the front as usual. To watch the back, turn the whole book over like turning a magazine around, so the binding is now in your other hand, and flip again. The back counts up 1, 2, 3… from that side.
  4. Split animations: the story carries on across the turn. After the last front slip, flip the book over and the next number picks up from the back.

Cutting on a Silhouette

  1. On the Make tab, tick Cut on a Silhouette machine, then Download Silhouette cut kit. The zip has a picture and a DXF cut file for each sheet, plus these steps.
  2. In Silhouette Studio’s Page Setup: Letter or A4, portrait, Registration Marks on (Type 1 for Cameo/Portrait), default positions.
  3. Merge in sheet-01.png, set it to the full page size, and position it at X 0, Y 0.
  4. Merge in sheet-01-cut.dxf, set its width to the page width, and position it at X 0, Y 0. Its red frame lines up with the page edge; the blue lines should sit on the slip edges.
  5. Print from Studio (so the registration marks print), put the sheet on the mat, and in the Send panel cut blue only, with red set to no cut.

Works in the free Basic Edition, which opens DXF but not SVG. Print & cut can land about 1/16″ off, so printed border lines are turned off in this mode.

Why the held frames? The first and last few slips sit against the cover and back and flash by while your thumb finds its grip, so a few repeats at each end let the real motion start and finish cleanly.
